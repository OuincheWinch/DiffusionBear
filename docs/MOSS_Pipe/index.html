MOSS-Image 5B on Apple Silicon: SFT vs DMD Distillation Benchmark

👤 By Research Team 📅 October 19, 2026 💻 Hardware: Apple Silicon M1 16GB Unified Memory 🧠 Text encoder: Qwen 3.5 9B — MLX 8-bit (first GPU MLX runtime)

🐻 1. One prompt, one seed, three inference pipelines

A single press-kit poster — a structured layout (virtual bounding boxes: alpine-lake background, wortmark, logo slot, version line, engine credits) — was rendered by the same MOSS-Image SFT 5B checkpoint through the three pipelines DiffusionBear supports. Identical seed (1024), identical resolution (768×512), identical layout JSON, identical text-encoder path. Only the sampler schedule and the CFG handling change:

  • Stock SFT (30 steps, CFG 3.0): the reference pipeline. Flow-matching Euler over a discrete schedule, two transformer forwards per step (conditional + unconditional). Maximum detail budget, maximum wall time (~43–46 min per poster).
  • DMD 8-step (lodestones adapter): the Distribution Matching Distillation adapter replaces the sampling schedule with 8 learned dataspace codes and folds the t=1 CFG vector into a fp32 time embedder — one forward per step, no unconditional pass (~13–14 min).
  • DMD 4-step (darask0 adapter): the same machinery at 4 codes: fastest MOSS arm (~7.5–8.5 min), the most compressed trajectory.

Distillation adapters do not merely skip steps: each arm is a genuinely different sampler (different trajectory family, CFG-free vs CFG-on), like our earlier Krea-2 4/6/8-step finding. All comparisons below must be read as pipeline comparisons, not quality gradations of one pipeline.

🔬 2. What every arm shared

  • Checkpoint: OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Image-SFT 5B (DiT), bf16, MLX.
  • Text encoder: Qwen 3.5 9B running on GPU through our MLX 8-bit conversion (g64, GDN layers kept bf16) — parity 0.9995 cos vs the torch bf16 reference (full write-up), ~17s per prompt instead of ~61s on CPU.
  • Structured layout: virtual bounding boxes serialized into the official summary/items JSON layout (abs_centralized, yxyx): full-surface background box, bold wordmark, logo slot, "V 0.4.1 beta" + "now runs", engine credits in the bottom corners.
  • Seed 1024, 768×512, FLUX.2-family fp32 VAE decode, no fast-VAE.
  • *s/step caveat: DMD arms carry ~2-3 min of fixed overhead (text-encoder pass, 252 LoRA wraps, adapter load) that dilutes their per-step average; the stock arm pays two forwards per step instead.

🚀 Practical takeaways

In-app, DiffusionBear auto-selects the DMD adapters for runs ≤ 8 steps (4 → darask0, 5–8 → lodestones) and keeps stock trajectories above. For poster work where text on the layout matters most, the 30-step stock arm renders the most stable lettering; for fast iteration the 4-step arm is 5.7× cheaper. All arms run 100% locally, offline, on a single 16 GB M1 — no cloud, no subscription.
